Quarantine and behavior
------
Supervisor: Dr. Bryce Morsky

Social dynamics are key to studying how diseases spread within a population. This project expands on traditional SIR models by modeling how likely an individual is to get vaccinated or participate in non-pharmaceutical interventions. This model can incorporate social cues, like the cost of vaccination or the social pressure to wear a mask, and how that effects the progression of the disease in a population.

Morphological Features of Murine Pulmonary Arterial Networks
------
Supervisors: Dr. Mette Olufsen, Dr. Radmila Sazdanovic

Hypoxia-induced pulmonary hypertension (HPH) is an incurable and life threatening disease, linked to disorders like COPD. Diagnosis of HPH is invasive, but advances in CT imaging offer a less invasive alternative. This exploratory study uses Topological Data Analysis, Shape Analysis, and the extraction of key geometric features to study how morphology of mice lung change in disease. This framework creates an adaptable pipeline going from micro-CT imaging to feature extraction to classification that can be applied to other organ/disease models.

Spherical Model for Dynamic Radial Deformation of Viscoelstic Microgels
------
Supervisor: Dr. Mansoor Haider

Platelet-like particles (PLPs) are synthetic microgels capable of aiding wound healing. Previous research identified that these particles deform under ultrasound stimulation. This project developed a three-dimensional viscoelastic model modeling the PLP under acoustic forcing. The resulting partial differential equation includes parameters ground in physical attributes of the PLPs that can be tuned to desired outcomes.
